# Originating project lessons

These historical examples explain design choices in two educational sites. They are not required reading, templates, or claims about current deployments.

## Separate intelligence and search

The introduction needed readers to understand the distinction between an assistant and research tools before making setup decisions. Moving secondary actions into navigation reduced competition with that explanation. A larger cookbook benefited from search, filtering, expandable examples, and stable anchors; the short introduction did not need those features.

Transferable lesson: choose actions and navigation for the reader's immediate question and the amount of content. A catalogue visitor may reasonably want installation much sooner.

## Portable LLM Council

The overview explained the method, offered a concrete example, and then led toward setup. A full sample could open separately so readers could compare it with the overview. The sample distinguished a fictional scenario, actual returned outputs, and editorial summaries. Builders reused maintained prompts and outputs rather than manually duplicating them.

Transferable lesson: show enough of the result to make its value concrete, preserve evidence labels, and avoid source drift. Neither a separate sample page nor new-tab navigation is compulsory for other sites.

## Apply selectively

Carry over useful principles, not repository URLs, provider-specific content, council methodology, exact colours, or page counts. Do not modify the originating sites as an implicit part of applying these lessons elsewhere.
