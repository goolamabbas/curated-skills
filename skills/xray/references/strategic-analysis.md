# Strategic analysis

Use the relevant lenses below when deeper comparison of explanations, strategic responses, or power shifts could change the assessment. They need not appear as separate output sections.

## Competing hypotheses

Compare distinct, plausible explanations supported by the story and available evidence. Include a credible ordinary explanation, such as routine process, error, or coincidence, where applicable. Do not manufacture an implausible alternative to meet a quota.

Assess which material evidence supports, contradicts, or fails to distinguish each explanation. Rank provisionally and identify the decisive evidence and unresolved gaps. Several explanations may survive; do not force one winner.

## Strategic interaction

Explain the relevant actors' incentives, constraints, and possible responses. Use game-theory labels only if useful; zero-sum describes payoffs, repeated describes interaction over time, and chicken or coordination describe strategic structures, so labels can overlap.

Assess a signal's credibility through incentives, verifiability, reputation, and cost. Low cost alone does not make it meaningless. Describe a plausible outcome and the assumptions it depends on; allow uncertainty, bounded rationality, and no useful game model.

## Power mapping

Identify any diplomatic, informational, military, economic, or other relevant lever that changed. Separate observed consequences from possible reactions and longer-term effects. Trace a concrete causal mechanism for each projected effect; do not assume a third-order effect exists or is the real story.
